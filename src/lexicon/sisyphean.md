Denoting a task that can never be completed, requiring endless, repetitive labor that rolls back to the starting line the moment progress is made.

The term originates from Greek mythology. King Sisyphus of Corinth was condemned by the gods to roll an immense boulder up a steep hill for eternity. Each time he reached the summit, the weight of the stone forced it to tumble back down into the plains, obliging him to descend and begin the climb all over again.

In everyday engineering and knowledge work, sisyphean tasks are routine: chasing zero inbox, resolving endless merge conflicts, maintaining fast-decaying dependencies, and pruning tech debt that regrows overnight. 

Yet as Albert Camus observed in *The Myth of Sisyphus*, recognizing the futility doesn't have to breed despair. The constant push against the boulder is where craft, patience, and purpose reside: *"The struggle itself toward the heights is enough to fill a man's heart. One must imagine Sisyphus happy."*
