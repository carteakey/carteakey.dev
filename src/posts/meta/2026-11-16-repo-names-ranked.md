My hobby is naming stuff, I guess. I have around sixty repos on GitHub and most of them have a name I picked because it made me smile.

So I asked Claude to rank them. It only had the names and whether each repo is public or private, and for most of them it had no idea what's inside. That's the point. A good name should work before you know what it's for.

## Claude's top 10

1. garminageddon: Claude called it the best pun I have. It's long, but you can read it in one go.
2. tokemon: token plus Pokémon, one letter changed, nothing to explain.
3. skillrex: works as T-rex and as Skrillex.
4. mcpuff: turns a dry acronym (MCP) into something cute.
5. muninn: Odin's raven of memory. A quiet reference rather than a joke, and it looks nice written down.
6. [wikibones](/blog/meta/wikibones/): odd enough that you want to know what it is.
7. vault-76: obvious to anyone who's played Fallout, and it sounds like a place you keep things. It's [my Obsidian vault](/blog/vault-76-my-obsidian-vault-structure/), so that one works.
8. sortling: small and friendly, and it says what it does. It started life as the Unforwarder and [got renamed](/blog/sortling/goodbye-unforwarder-hello-sortling/).
9. qualms: a real word with a pun probably hiding in it. Claude said that's the hardest kind to find.
10. placedex: the `-dex` suffix fits, because it really is a dex of places.

Honourable mentions went to chirptype, nanoghibli and alvidaa.

## The boring tier

The bottom of the list was every repo named after what it does: `ml-experiments`, `data-annotation`, `server-compose`. They're fine as labels, but nobody's going to remember them.

## Renaming things that were fine

This week I renamed two repos to `-dex` names so they'd match Placedex, then renamed both of them back.

The `-dex` suffix only works when the thing is actually a collection you fill in, like a Pokédex. Placedex is one. The other two weren't, and their old names were already right.
