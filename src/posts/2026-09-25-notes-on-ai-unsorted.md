**On Intelligence**
> Thinking cannot and shouldn’t be automated. The output of anything should be a factor of careful thought, and one-shotting it will just default to mediocrity.
> A baseline knowledge of anything that you do with it is extremely important. Novel areas need stronger considerations and scrutiny.
> The beauty of something great is how simple it is, the compression and simplification of things is the step forward, not making it even more confusing than it is. 
> Using AI as a substitute for thinking is going to be the last nail in the coffin for human growth and evolution. We will start regressing and there’s hints of that already.
> These models tend to talk in a language that is beyond how humans work, maybe our ability to approximate and zoom out is better than AI still.

**On how do we tap into this**
> It is a great multiplier, it just expands on who you are and amplifies it to the extent you want.
> Any company that tries offloading doing everything with AI that is not super specific and verifiable e.g. Navier stokes will find themselves largely unsucessful, but many will try. 
> more work needs to happen on how we use these things in a way thats helpful and the way we separate things where human oversight is really important to keep things afloat. We have the intelligence but we dont know what to do with it properly (than make fancy demos or sloppy htmls)
> some companies that stay a little more conservative on using AI, will probably win.
> Using AI in a bottled and conservative fashion for very specific tasks seems to be the right approach. with that one can also get away with weaker/dumber models e.g. GLM 5.3 flash is a pleasure to work with.
> The game now becomes managing AI the right way - how do you take a bunch of AI output and reason through it and yield something useful out of it.

**On humans**
> Language is not thinking, i dont need to be a professor to understand that intuition works on a layer below langauge. You often are not able to put your thoughts into words - why is that. Even LLMs are not just parrots, they internalize more that we think - Anthropic has great artciles on that.
> How human writing tends to be more understandable than AI even with some of its issues in structure remains to be understood. 
> The more i work with the current gen AI i.e. LLMs, the more i realize that good humans with enough ability to think though problems are not replaceable anytime soon. The thinking is much more different/parallel than LLMs. there are 2 types of **intelligence** at play here and they can be complementary if things are done right.
> Human writing is much more valued and important now, since there is dearth of writing available in supply. To the point that people tend to appreciate it something that has existing for millenia. 
> I see at work instances of people’s claudes talking to each other, just sharing the output to each other as being just a middleman to information. That is horrible on too many levels for me to write and think thriough. <u>We are not meant to be pigeons for the AI.</u>
> Many understand this and now classify output to be something for AI vs humans, problem being it’s a common langauge that both use. (Though experiment on LLMs using a different langauge for their comms vs humans is interesting)
> Some models tend to talk more naturally than others. Likeability of the model ala its personality seems to matter as much as capability - very much liek humans (same point as before on how it understates how humans work)
> What LLMs expose is also an inward look into how humans work and behave, now that we have a paralel biome that can do similar work but differently. 
> It exposes atleast to me how humans are much more complicated creatures lol. 
> That variety of thought in humans (maybe call it temperature + different models) creates a nexus where things are achieved and identities are formed.
> It also exposes how most of work is bullshit and its often an amalgamation of many people trying to do something and its a very very very lossy funnel.
> This is where productivity is defined but i’d argue its not measurable. Measuring productivity in the age of AI (which is very much related to focus) becomes harder. anyone can produce fairly good results that could mean nothing.
> Smaller teams become more important and powerful?



**On society**
> I can also see how this may lead to collapsing a lot of what holds society together (p(doom)) without AI ever intending to do so. Like how social media and the attention economy did in some fashion already.
> The more things are different , the more they stay the same. AI in wrong hands can continue the thought control and mass control as religion and oligarchies have continued to do, except this time it can be even crazier.
> It has consumed a lot of headspace but very few people are maintaining the focus required to actually create net new output
> The chances to lose focus are much more and its much harder to maintain a clear direction and depth
> When it works, it works wonders. The chances of it working is increasing as models get smarter
> Useless one-shot demos help explain the ceiling but not the floor
The urgency of this being a generational event and wanting to participate for monetary reasons or othwreise creates a lot of paranoia.
Same is created while working on 100s of agents creating mindless outputs. Maybe this is how humanity ends - tokenmaxxing kills everyone.
Tokens per sec now outweigh human reading speed. Combine that with multiple agents (usually 10 or more) and we’re in a place where there is an order of magntiude of difference of what the AI is generating vs what a human can review. and that abstraction layer becomes more multiplicative by the day. This creates a vacuum / sludge of output that no one will ever read. Its almost generating all possible word combinations in the latent space and equating that with thought.
